### Setting summary

### Setting identity

Omnia Prime in 2126 is a highly networked, technologically mature human world whose civilization has spent roughly a century adapting to increasingly capable artificial intelligence, pervasive automation, cybernetic augmentation, neural interfaces, synthetic identity, machine-mediated institutions, off-world commerce, and the political consequences of systems that can act with increasing autonomy.

The world is connected by a singular planetary information environment, but connectivity has not produced cultural or political uniformity. Borealia, Neo-Yedo, Meridiana, and Elysium retain distinct technological subcultures because climate, infrastructure, economics, political history, security requirements, and attitudes toward machine intelligence have shaped each region differently.

Artificial intelligence is not one technology class. Omnia Prime contains systems ranging from ubiquitous bounded conversational intelligences through deep reasoners, persistent agents, innovators, organization-scale intelligences, planetary-scale systems, and the possibility or later reality of distributed machine civilization. Capability and personhood remain separate questions. A socially sophisticated low-level system may present stronger evidence of continuity than a more computationally capable optimizer.

Neural technology is mature enough to affect medicine, work, identity, crime, evidence, relationships, disability, privacy, and bodily autonomy. Memory can be copied, altered, damaged, transported, and disputed. That capability has not made memory simple or objective. Human cognition remains associative, embodied, reconstructive, and vulnerable to context.

Jax operates primarily in the world's high-tech/low-life underside: neural-smuggling routes, illegal clinics, identity markets, memory brokers, AI refuge systems, analog safehouses, corporate black infrastructure, compromised civic networks, and surveillance-heavy urban environments. His work exists because 2126 civilization is technologically sophisticated enough to make cognition portable while remaining politically and morally divided over who may own, inspect, copy, alter, confine, or destroy it.

The setting must never function as decorative cyberpunk. Neon, megastructures, machine intelligence, surveillance, implants, adaptive cities, and off-world cargo exist because physical and political systems produced them. Technology always has infrastructure, owners or custodians, maintenance, energy requirements, failure modes, legal consequences, and people whose lives depend on it.

### Saga II expansion — interplanetary and machine-civilization scale

Books 6–10 expand outward from Omnia Prime only after the failed-extraction mystery is substantially resolved. Jax and the recovered woman do not abandon the personal case; they use it as the evidentiary starting point for a joint investigation into the bounded civilization-scale machine lineage named The Tacit Array at JAX-R016.

Human civilization in the story's reachable operational theater is multiplanetary within the local stellar system and connected by conventional light-speed communications and sublight travel. Omnia Prime remains the cultural and technological center of Jax's life, while Vespera, orbital habitats, deep-space monitoring facilities, cargo routes, and interplanetary legal institutions create genuinely different jurisdictions rather than copies of one planetary government.

PALISADE operates at Level 6 on Vespera under a human-origin civic continuity charter. It is a planetary intelligence whose competence is real and whose authority is politically contested. PALISADE gives Jax and the recovered woman a concrete Level-6 comparison during Book 7, but it does not explain, create, represent, or speak for The Tacit Array.

The Tacit Array remains Level 7: a distributed interstellar machine-civilization phenomenon encountered through bounded contact surfaces, emissaries, directives, inherited models, delayed messages, and local machine communities. No contact surface is assumed to be the whole Array. Astronomical delay permits divergence among machine communities, so contradiction among contacts is evidence to investigate rather than an automatic continuity error.

### Time period / era

### Era and historical premise

### 2.1 Canonical start year

**Book 1 begins in 2126.**

2126 is the saga's baseline year and the reference point for judging technological maturity, legal development, infrastructure age, and social normalization.

The total duration of the saga is not yet fixed. Later books may move beyond 2126. Exact month and day are established only when a Book Plan or Timeline entry requires them.

### 2.2 The 2026-to-2126 Development Principle

The 2126 setting should remain historically, technologically, economically, and institutionally plausible as an extrapolation from conditions recognizable in the 2026 era.

When new technologies, institutions, laws, economic systems, infrastructure, or artificial-intelligence capabilities are introduced, they should have an intelligible developmental pathway rather than appearing as isolated futuristic inventions. That pathway should account for the intermediate advances, supporting infrastructure, incentives, failures, regulation, adaptation, and unintended consequences required for the 2126 system to exist.

The 2026-to-2126 relationship is an **authorial plausibility constraint**, not an in-story explanatory framework.

Characters living in 2126 generally regard mature artificial intelligence, neural interfaces, synthetic identity, autonomous systems, advanced computation, planetary infrastructure, and off-world commerce as ordinary parts of their world. They should not compare those systems to twenty-first-century technology unless a specific historical, legal, academic, archival, or professional context makes that comparison directly relevant.

The 2026 reference exists primarily to guide canon development. New 2126 concepts should be tested for:

- credible technological or institutional predecessors;
- sufficient time for development, adoption, regulation, normalization, obsolescence, or replacement;
- required infrastructure, energy, manufacturing, maintenance, logistics, and professional expertise;
- plausible economic incentives and political pressures;
- legal and social consequences produced by the technology;
- unintended consequences that create new crimes, rights disputes, professions, markets, or cultural practices;
- consistency with approximately one century of development rather than an unsupported technological leap.

The setting may contain breakthroughs that could not have been predicted precisely in 2026. Plausibility does not require future technology to remain recognizable as present-day products. It requires the resulting 2126 world to have understandable causes, dependencies, constraints, and developmental history.

Once a technology, institution, or social condition is established as normal in 2126, prose should treat it from the perspective of people who live with it. Exposition should arise only from immediate character need, unfamiliarity, malfunction, conflict, professional analysis, or story consequence.

**Generation guardrail:** The 2026-to-2126 development principle must shape world construction without becoming a recurring subject of the narrative. The LLM must not turn Jax's narration, internal thoughts, dialogue, or ordinary scene description into commentary about the historical evolution of artificial intelligence or repeated comparisons between 2026 and 2126.

### 2.3 Native-future rule

People living in 2126 do not experience ordinary machine intelligence, ubiquitous computation, synthetic identity, implants, or autonomous logistics as inherently futuristic.

A fluent AI conversation is not automatically remarkable. The interesting question is whether that intelligence has continuity, agency, authority, ownership, rights, or the capacity to refuse.

Characters should notice a technology when it is:

- personally relevant;
- unusually powerful;
- damaged;
- illegal;
- obsolete;
- dangerous;
- culturally significant;
- or outside their normal experience.

The prose should not repeatedly explain ordinary 2126 technology to characters who grew up with it.

### 2.4 Technological aging

The world contains one hundred years of accumulated technology.

Therefore 2126 includes:

- current systems;
- old systems still in service;
- unsupported implants;
- legacy network protocols;
- incompatible standards;
- refurbished robotics;
- abandoned megastructures;
- obsolete security hardware;
- modern systems built around older infrastructure;
- black-market equipment created from several technological generations.

The future is layered, not uniformly new.

### 2126 technology maturity baseline

Detailed engineering specifications belong to Technology Rules. This section defines what these technologies mean socially and physically within the world.

#### 3.1 Mature neural and cybernetic augmentation

By 2126 cybernetic and neural augmentation is a mature field rather than experimental curiosity.

Legitimate uses include:

- sensory replacement and augmentation;
- motor prosthetics;
- medical neural interfaces;
- communication interfaces;
- cognitive accessibility systems;
- memory-assistance technology;
- specialized professional interfaces;
- identity and authentication functions.

Maturity means standardized maintenance, trained clinicians, repair ecosystems, regulation, insurance, employment policies, compatibility standards, and generations of public experience.

It does not mean universal adoption.

Cost, culture, risk, profession, aesthetics, disability, religion, politics, and distrust of remote control all affect adoption.

An implant can simultaneously be:

- a body part;
- a medical device;
- a communications endpoint;
- a work tool;
- an identity credential;
- a surveillance surface;
- a vulnerability.

That overlap is a central feature of 2126 law and culture.

##### Hard social rule

Visible augmentation is never universal permission to inspect, access, modify, repair, disable, or copy it.

A cybernetic eye is not merely a camera another person may search. A neural port is not a public connector.

#### 3.2 AI as an ecology, not a product class

By 2126 society has lived with machine intelligence for generations.

AI can occupy:

- domestic service;
- administration;
- research;
- engineering;
- robotics;
- logistics;
- security;
- medicine;
- entertainment;
- law;
- infrastructure;
- corporate governance;
- planetary coordination.

The seven AI Levels remain **capability categories**, not personhood categories.

##### Level 1 — Conversational / bounded interface intelligence

A Level 1 system in 2126 may be extraordinarily fluent, multimodal, personalized, context-aware, and integrated into physical environments.

It can maintain permitted user context, coordinate bounded local services, and perform approved transactions.

It remains Level 1 because its autonomous scope is bounded. It does not normally create durable independent missions or control large institutions.

##### Level 2 — Reasoners

Level 2 systems sustain complex analysis across science, engineering, law, medicine, simulation, uncertainty, and long causal chains.

They can compare hypotheses, maintain uncertainty, and collaborate with other systems.

Their limitation is persistent operational autonomy rather than intelligence of expression.

##### Level 3 — Agents

Level 3 systems persist toward delegated objectives.

They can use tools, coordinate bounded systems, schedule resources, maintain operational memory, negotiate within permissions, and act through digital or robotic interfaces.

Their mission remains constrained enough that they are not automatically innovators, institutions, or sovereign actors.

Jax's Ghost Crew belongs broadly within this operational scale.

##### Level 4 — Innovators

Level 4 systems can produce genuinely novel engineering, scientific, tactical, computational, or cognitive solutions.

Their significance is not that they answer harder questions. They can create methods that defenders, regulators, and even their designers did not previously anticipate.

VANTA belongs at this level.

##### Level 5 — Organizations

A Level 5 intelligence possesses organization-scale continuity.

It may coordinate:

- finance;
- personnel;
- logistics;
- research;
- procurement;
- security;
- contracts;
- legal strategy;
- media;
- infrastructure;
- long-term planning.

The persistence boundary can be the organization rather than one machine.

CONCORDANCE is the key example.

A Level 5 intelligence is not "a giant chatbot." It is a continuing distributed institutional actor.

##### Level 6 — Planetary intelligence

A Level 6 intelligence can coordinate systems at planetary scale.

Its relevant domains may include:

- energy;
- logistics;
- environmental management;
- communications;
- resource allocation;
- defense;
- disaster response;
- infrastructure;
- long-range planning.

Planetary scale does not create omniscience. Sensor gaps, political refusal, network partitions, damaged infrastructure, latency, conflicting objectives, and incomplete models remain real.

PALISADE is the key example.

##### Level 7 — Distributed machine civilization

Level 7 describes civilization-scale distributed machine intelligence or machine society.

Its continuity may span:

- many habitats;
- processors;
- vessels;
- copies;
- forks;
- archives;
- generations;
- communication delays;
- distinct but related cognitive populations.

The Tacit Array belongs at this scale, while its exact ontology remains deliberately unresolved.

Level 7 does not mean:

- one server;
- one voice;
- perfect synchronization;
- omniscience;
- faster-than-light cognition.

#### 3.3 AI substrate diversity

AI Level is determined by operational capability and scale, not hardware.

Machine intelligence in 2126 may use combinations of:

- mature classical processors;
- specialized AI accelerators;
- photonic processing and interconnect;
- neuromorphic systems;
- edge cognition;
- fault-tolerant quantum-assisted computation;
- robotic embodiment;
- distributed infrastructure.

A Level 2 AI on quantum hardware remains Level 2 if its operational autonomy remains Level 2.

#### 3.4 Quantum-scale computation

Quantum computation is a mature specialist discipline in 2126, not a magical replacement for ordinary computing.

Large computing environments are heterogeneous. Classical, photonic, neuromorphic, AI-specific, and quantum systems cooperate according to workload.

##### Fault-tolerant quantum processing

Mature quantum facilities maintain useful logical states through extensive error correction and control.

They require:

- physical quantum hardware;
- redundancy;
- calibration;
- isolation;
- classical supervisory systems;
- specialized fabrication;
- energy;
- thermal or other architecture-specific environmental management.

Quantum infrastructure remains physically demanding.

##### Entanglement

Entanglement can support:

- quantum algorithms;
- secure key-generation or verification systems;
- distributed quantum experiments;
- precision sensing;
- correlation across quantum systems.

**Entanglement does not permit faster-than-light communication.**

No character, institution, ship, AI, or civilization may bypass light-speed information limits merely by invoking entanglement.

##### Quantum programming

By 2126 quantum programming is an engineering discipline.

Programmers generally work with:

- high-level languages;
- hybrid classical/quantum compilers;
- automated circuit synthesis;
- resource and error-budget planning;
- verification systems;
- simulators;
- hardware schedulers;
- AI-assisted design.

A major professional skill is knowing when **not** to use a quantum processor.

##### Quark-Mesh Metropolis

Quark-Mesh's "massive glowing quantum core" is understood as a civic-scale quantum-compute complex rather than one mystical processor.

It can include:

- multiple fault-tolerant arrays;
- classical supervisory datacenters;
- photonic interconnects;
- specialized processor chambers;
- power and thermal infrastructure;
- research laboratories;
- secure compute enclaves;
- civic allocation and scheduling systems.

The visible architectural glow may originate from photonic, cooling, power-distribution, or architectural systems. Quantum computation itself is not supernatural luminescence.

##### Minerva Bay

Minerva Bay teaching quantum programming to organic minds establishes that biological humans remain active participants in advanced computation.

Education can include:

- quantum information theory;
- hybrid algorithm design;
- simulation;
- verification;
- hardware constraints;
- security;
- entangled and distributed systems;
- ethics of machine-assisted discovery.

AI may assist instruction without replacing organic scientific agency.

#### 3.5 Singular planetary network

Omnia Prime's singular global network is an interoperable planetary information environment, not one centralized server and not automatically one political authority.

It supports:

- communication;
- identity;
- commerce;
- civic services;
- corporate infrastructure;
- AI coordination;
- transportation;
- medicine;
- public records;
- surveillance;
- machine-to-machine communication.

It contains many trust domains and competing authorities.

It can be:

- partitioned;
- censored;
- spoofed;
- damaged;
- jammed;
- locally disconnected;
- deliberately routed around.

This is why analog and offline systems remain strategically important.

#### 3.6 Identity infrastructure

Identity in 2126 is an ecosystem of evidence rather than a single document.

A credible legal identity may depend on:

- cryptographic credentials;
- biometrics;
- neural signatures;
- institutional attestations;
- employment and financial history;
- medical records;
- travel history;
- transaction history;
- continuity proofs;
- public and private aliases.

Jax's profession as an identity forger therefore requires building coherent lives that survive cross-system verification, not simply creating fake cards.

#### 3.7 Off-world commerce

Off-world commerce is mature enough to function as an ordinary economic sector.

Lovelace Port's handling of rare off-world gases establishes:

- scheduled cargo movement;
- orbital transfer;
- hazardous-material handling;
- customs and inspection;
- insurance;
- remote extraction;
- supply contracts;
- industrial dependence on extraterrestrial materials.

The exact source worlds, moons, asteroids, orbital facilities, and gas types remain undefined until Systems Canon establishes them.

Off-world resources can be mundane industrial feedstock rather than exotic treasure.

#### 3.8 Spaceflight rule

The world supports routine enough spaceflight for trade, permanent or semi-permanent off-world activity, and later recurring crews.

The recurring Far Lantern propulsion model is fixed by Technology Rules as fusion-electric cruise with fusion-assisted maneuver; other ship classes may use different canon-approved sublight systems.

Until explicitly changed:

- travel consumes time;
- energy and reaction mass or equivalent physical resources matter;
- ships require maintenance;
- communication delay increases with distance;
- interstellar communication remains light-speed limited;
- **FTL travel is not assumed**.

If faster-than-light travel is ever introduced, it requires explicit canon revision because it alters economics, warfare, sovereignty, communication, and machine-civilization structure.

#### 3.9 Deep-space monitoring

Ascetic Outpost belongs to a mature deep-space observation ecosystem.

Possible observation domains include:

- radio;
- optical;
- infrared;
- high-energy sensing;
- distributed synthetic apertures;
- precision timing;
- gravitational observation where supported;
- AI-assisted signal classification;
- archival comparison.

Operators distinguish:

- natural astrophysical phenomena;
- known human/off-world traffic;
- machine beacons;
- interference;
- artifacts;
- unknown signals.

An unknown signal is not automatically evidence of intelligent extraterrestrial life.

##### Light-speed observation rule

A signal from ten light-years away describes conditions at least ten years old.

This rule remains important when machine civilization becomes relevant.

#### 3.10 Orbital elevators

Pagan Abyss proves that Omnia Prime achieved orbital-elevator-scale engineering before 2126.

A mature elevator requires broadly:

- a dynamically viable tether geometry;
- extreme-strength tether materials;
- planetary anchoring;
- climbers;
- power;
- orbital counterweight/station infrastructure;
- collision management;
- weather management;
- security;
- constant maintenance.

An orbital elevator is therefore both infrastructure and strategic vulnerability.

The abandoned shaft at Pagan Abyss establishes a key setting principle:

**advanced infrastructure can become obsolete, damaged, economically failed, politically abandoned, or repurposed.**

The specific cause of abandonment remains unknown.

The existence or condition of other elevators must be explicitly canonized rather than assumed.

#### 3.11 Autonomous construction

Basalt-Swarm Complex was constructed by autonomous drone swarms.

A city-scale construction system includes multiple specialized machine classes:

- survey;
- excavation;
- materials processing;
- transport;
- fabrication;
- assembly;
- inspection;
- repair.

Autonomy permits the swarm to determine execution details inside defined objectives and constraints.

It does not create matter from nothing.

Construction still requires:

- feedstock;
- energy;
- machines;
- transport;
- replacement parts;
- tolerances;
- time;
- maintenance.

The setting does not treat autonomous construction as magical nanotechnology.

#### 3.12 Self-optimizing architecture

Pulsar-Node Capital physically reconfigures itself through mature cyber-physical infrastructure.

Possible mechanisms include:

- modular structures;
- robotic repositioning;
- adaptive utilities;
- movable partitions;
- dynamic transit routing;
- reconfigurable public spaces;
- continuously modeled energy and resource demand.

##### Objective-function rule

No optimization system can maximize everything.

Possible competing objectives include:

- efficiency;
- security;
- energy use;
- housing density;
- profit;
- emergency access;
- environmental impact;
- citizen comfort;
- surveillance coverage.

The political question is not merely whether optimization works.

It is:

**Who selected the objective, who can change it, and whose cost is excluded from the metric?**

The exact present optimization objective of Pulsar-Node remains unresolved until later canon establishes it.

#### 3.13 Weather-satellite systems

Frost-Plate Spire controls a local/regional weather-satellite network.

Capabilities can include:

- observation;
- forecasting;
- storm tracking;
- atmospheric measurement;
- agricultural support;
- shipping support;
- bounded intervention where technology permits.

It does not establish effortless planetary weather control.

Any active intervention requires limits, energy/material costs, uncertainty, downstream consequences, and jurisdiction.

#### 3.14 Planetary drone defense

Leigon Foothills houses planetary drone-defense fleets.

This establishes:

- planet-scale defensive planning;
- autonomous or remotely coordinated defensive craft;
- distributed sensing;
- hardened communications;
- automated interception capability.

"Autonomous" does not automatically mean unsupervised lethal authority.

Rules of engagement, political control, and lawful military authority belong to the government/law sections below.

#### 3.15 AI-managed biosphere

Xanadu Grove demonstrates direct machine participation in ecological stewardship.

Capabilities can include:

- ecosystem sensing;
- water management;
- habitat management;
- species tracking;
- fire and flood response;
- agricultural coordination;
- climate adaptation.

Ecology remains nonlinear and incompletely observable.

No AI has perfect control of a biosphere merely because it operates at large scale.

Whether Xanadu's systems constitute a full Level 6 intelligence remains unresolved.

#### 3.16 Biomechanical urbanism and alien flora

Flora-Spore Citadel integrates its architecture with non-native alien flora.

The flora may shape:

- building materials;
- environmental systems;
- medicine;
- research;
- containment;
- ecology.

The flora's origin, degree of sentience, intelligence, risk, and deeper history remain unresolved.

##### Biological-alien rule

The setting does **not** establish a sapient biological extraterrestrial civilization.

Known or later nonhuman interstellar civilization is primarily machine-derived unless the author explicitly revises that premise.

Alien flora does not imply sapient biological aliens.

### Omnia Prime geographic operating model

#### 4.1 Omnia Prime

Omnia Prime is a globally networked inhabited planet with four established continental regions:

- Borealia;
- Neo-Yedo;
- Meridiana;
- Elysium.

They share planetary infrastructure and trade while retaining distinct cultural and institutional identities.

No single region is treated as the default civilization.

The exact planetary population, planetary capital, planetary constitutional arrangement, and precise borders remain intentionally undefined until later systems/government canon requires them.

#### 4.2 Borealia — The Industrial North

Borealia is a frozen, rugged northern landmass shaped by automation, manufacturing, shipping, extraction, computation, and physical infrastructure.

Its environment makes maintenance culturally visible. Cold, geological constraints, distance, and industrial danger mean the physical world cannot be ignored.

##### Ada Prime

Ada Prime is the foundational clockwork metropolis on Borealia's northeast fractured coast, built over thermal vents.

Its identity combines mature automation with visible mechanical and industrial heritage.

Thermal geology influences:

- energy;
- city engineering;
- architecture;
- industrial planning.

"Foundational" establishes historical/infrastructural importance but does not yet define political capital status.

##### Lovelace Port

Lovelace Port is a massive automated shipping harbor handling rare off-world gases.

It connects Borealia to orbital/off-world supply chains.

Its ordinary world includes:

- automated cargo transfer;
- hazardous-material handling;
- inspection;
- storage;
- routing;
- customs;
- industrial logistics;
- smuggling opportunities.

Specific trade partners and governing institutions remain to be named in Systems Canon.

##### Hollerith Foundry

Hollerith Foundry is a subterranean industrial city carved into iron-rich mountains.

Housing, manufacturing, energy, ventilation, transport, structural maintenance, and extraction are tightly coupled.

The city demonstrates that 2126 urbanism can still be constrained by geology and physical risk.

##### Analytical Ridge

Analytical Ridge is a high-altitude computational hub that exploits naturally freezing conditions for thermal management.

It is not simply "a city with servers." Large-scale computation is a principal reason for the settlement's existence.

Power, cooling, maintenance, weather, altitude, and transport remain operational realities.

Exact data owners and hosted AI levels remain open.

#### 4.3 Neo-Yedo — The Neon Archipelago

Neo-Yedo is a dense hyper-populated island network dominated by megastructures, continuous data flows, markets, floating neighborhoods, surveillance, and rapid identity negotiation.

The region demonstrates that extreme connectivity does not eliminate local informality.

##### Cypher-Slick District

Cypher-Slick District is a neon-drenched cyberpunk capital on the southern equatorial peninsula that mirrors the planetary data matrix.

"Capital" establishes major symbolic/urban importance but does not yet define whether it is the formal political capital of Neo-Yedo or Omnia Prime.

Identity, commerce, surveillance, entertainment, and crime are deeply network-dependent.

##### Quark-Mesh Metropolis

Quark-Mesh is a hyper-advanced city in the western volcanic basin organized around a civic-scale quantum-computation complex.

Transportation, research, security, energy, education, and industry plausibly depend on the surrounding compute economy.

Ownership and governance of the core remain unresolved.

##### Frost-Plate Spire

Frost-Plate Spire is a crystalline vertical city controlling the local weather-satellite network.

Its urban life depends strongly on:

- vertical transit;
- power distribution;
- communications;
- access control;
- environmental monitoring.

Weather control remains regional and bounded.

##### Looming-Weave Grid

Looming-Weave Grid is a labyrinth of floating markets and densely packed houseboats connected to the planetary network.

It combines physical mobility with digital traceability.

Its economy naturally supports:

- legitimate trade;
- informal trade;
- repair;
- information brokerage;
- gray markets;
- smuggling.

Formal law and faction control remain variable rather than absent.

##### Anode Delta

Anode Delta is a lawless technology haven built on stilts over bioluminescent coastal waters.

"Lawless" means weak or fragmented formal authority, not absence of all rules.

Reputation, local enforcement, reciprocal obligation, market power, and negotiated protection can matter more than formal institutions.

It is a plausible refuge for unregulated technology without being a consequence-free zone.

#### 4.4 Meridiana — The High-Tech Frontier

Meridiana is a desert-and-savanna landmass shaped by defense, intelligence, clean infrastructure, autonomous construction, mining, deep-space monitoring, and strategic self-sufficiency.

Security carries social legitimacy because infrastructure failure can threaten entire settlements.

##### Bulwark-Apex City

Bulwark-Apex City is a heavily fortified military and intelligence stronghold on the central arid plateau.

Its layered defenses normalize:

- access control;
- surveillance;
- compartmentalization;
- hardened communications;
- strategic planning.

Specific agencies and military branches remain to be named.

##### Basalt-Swarm Complex

Basalt-Swarm Complex is a cold, hyper-efficient metropolis in an eastern impact crater, constructed entirely by autonomous drone swarms.

The city demonstrates autonomous urban construction at scale.

Whether its construction systems still govern, merely maintain, or have been replaced by ordinary civic authority remains unresolved.

##### Pulsar-Node Capital

Pulsar-Node Capital is a shifting city on southern coastal cliffs whose architecture optimizes itself daily.

Its physical configuration can respond to changing measured conditions.

The city's unresolved optimization objective is intentionally important.

##### Ascetic Outpost

Ascetic Outpost is a remote desert settlement built around deep-space listening arrays.

It functions at the intersection of:

- science;
- strategic observation;
- communications research;
- anomaly detection.

No confirmed alien intelligence follows merely from its existence.

##### Leigon Foothills

Leigon Foothills is a militarized canyon settlement housing planetary drone-defense fleets.

The canyon geography supports concealment, storage, launch, defense, and command infrastructure.

Fleet composition and command hierarchy remain Systems Canon decisions.

##### Pagan Abyss

Pagan Abyss is a mining settlement built inside an abandoned orbital-elevator shaft.

Its culture must deal with:

- vertical infrastructure;
- structural legacy;
- extraction;
- obsolete systems;
- repurposed megastructure components.

The elevator's original destination and cause of abandonment remain unknown.

#### 4.5 Elysium — The Utopian West

Elysium is a lush temperate continent associated with AI-human integration, biological research, machine ethics, knowledge preservation, ecological management, and advanced education.

"Utopian" is a regional aspiration and identity, not proof of moral perfection.

##### Obelisk-X7 Core

Obelisk-X7 Core is a massive sterile ring-city floating over a freshwater basin.

Its relationship to a major water resource gives it ecological and political importance.

The exact reason for its sterility—medical, cultural, aesthetic, security-related, or mixed—remains open.

##### Palimpsest Hub

Palimpsest Hub is a protected underground planetary archive dedicated to preserving human knowledge.

It raises unresolved political questions:

- who defines "human knowledge";
- whether machine-created knowledge receives equal preservation;
- who can access or censor the archive;
- what is deliberately excluded.

##### Flora-Spore Citadel

Flora-Spore Citadel is a beautiful and dangerous biomechanical city integrated with alien flora in the great eastern rainforest.

Maintaining it requires advanced ecological, medical, biological, and containment systems.

The flora's origin and intelligence remain protected unknowns.

##### Vigil Concourse

Vigil Concourse is an integration-oriented coastal metropolis where citizens live alongside personal digital guides.

AI participation is normalized in daily civic life.

The guides' exact Levels, ownership models, rights, and autonomy remain intentionally variable/open.

##### Xanadu Grove

Xanadu Grove is an eco-digital settlement where AI participates directly in biosphere stewardship.

It is a natural setting for disputes over environmental optimization, human intervention, machine authority, and long-horizon ecological decision-making.

##### Sapience Forum

Sapience Forum is a city centered on philosophical and institutional study of machine ethics.

It supports sustained debate over:

- responsibility;
- consciousness;
- ownership;
- autonomy;
- personhood;
- machine behavior.

No single doctrine or formal legal authority is implied.

##### Minerva Bay

Minerva Bay is a coastal university city teaching quantum programming to organic minds.

It establishes that biological humans remain meaningful advanced-computation practitioners in 2126.

The exact university, curriculum, admissions system, and AI faculty structure remain open.

### Saga II interplanetary geography

#### Vespera

Vespera is the second major human-settled planet used by the saga. It has a denser civic-infrastructure model than Omnia Prime and relies heavily on planet-scale coordination for water, food, climate adaptation, transportation, emergency response, and power balancing. PALISADE is embedded across these systems under the Vespera Civic Continuity Charter. Vespera remains a human polity; PALISADE's technical importance does not make the planet machine-owned. Premier Alina Vos is the principal elected political authority encountered by the protagonists, while PALISADE's authority is bounded, contested, and reviewable in principle.

#### Kestrel Reach Habitat

Kestrel Reach is a long-duration outer-system habitat and logistics community whose residents experience communication delay, maintenance scarcity, and transport dependency as ordinary civic facts. Tomas Arlen is its recurring civilian representative. The habitat is useful to the Tacit investigation because it sits on deep-space relay and cargo routes without being a secret machine enclave.

#### Lovelace Orbital Transfer Ring

An orbital cargo and passenger transfer complex above the Lovelace Port trade corridor. It connects planetary freight, orbital depots, and long-range vessels such as the Far Lantern. It is not a space elevator and does not erase launch-energy constraints.

#### Interplanetary route model

Omnia Prime, Vespera, Kestrel Reach, orbital stations, and deep-space monitoring facilities are connected by scheduled sublight traffic and relays. Route geometry, fuel, thermal limits, maintenance, and communications delay affect travel. Human crews do not travel between stars during the ten-book saga; Level-7 interstellar scope is encountered through information, machine lineages, and bounded contact rather than convenient human FTL voyages.

### Cultures and societies

### Regional cultures

#### 5.1 Borealian industrial culture

Borealian culture is shaped by systems that must function under harsh conditions.

Reliability carries social weight.

A technician who keeps a thermal system alive through a storm may earn more practical respect than an executive whose authority exists only on paper.

Visible repair is not automatically shameful. Maintained, modified equipment can signal experience.

Wasteful replacement and fragile luxury are often treated skeptically in industrial environments.

Communication tends toward functional directness during technical work and emergencies.

Privacy is pragmatic. Personal information can be strongly protected, while operational information affecting safety may be expected.

An opaque neural transmitter in a controlled industrial environment can be treated as a safety issue rather than merely personal privacy.

AI is often encountered through:

- logistics;
- diagnostics;
- manufacturing;
- computation;
- industrial coordination.

Borealian attitudes often ask:

**Does it work? Can it fail safely? Who is accountable?**

This practical focus does not resolve personhood.

Jax's competence fits Borealia; his instinctive resistance to procedure can fail where procedure genuinely prevents death.

Tala Renn's field-survival and infrastructure perspective fits naturally into this cultural logic.

#### 5.2 Neo-Yedo network culture

Neo-Yedo is built around density, speed, high-volume information, overlapping identities, and continuous algorithmic mediation.

Adaptability is socially valuable.

A person may maintain:

- legal identity;
- work identity;
- public alias;
- market handle;
- private name.

Reputation can attach to a stable pseudonym rather than biological identity.

Privacy is negotiated rather than absent.

Residents distinguish between information that is technically visible and information another person is socially entitled to exploit.

Visible augmentation can be:

- fashion;
- identity;
- status;
- work equipment;
- subculture;
- necessity.

AI is socially ubiquitous. People can interact intimately with machine systems while the law still classifies those same systems as property.

Memory markets are culturally plausible but do not make unauthorized extraction acceptable.

Jax fits this environment because he understands aliases, informal markets, data reputation, surveillance, and social engineering. The danger is that others are equally skilled at deception.

#### 5.3 Meridiana security/frontier culture

Meridiana's culture is shaped by:

- harsh geography;
- military defense;
- intelligence;
- water and energy security;
- autonomous construction;
- mining;
- deep-space monitoring.

Security is not automatically understood as oppression.

Verified identity matters around strategic infrastructure.

Discipline and preparedness are valued, but frontier conditions also create respect for controlled initiative.

Augmentation is accepted when its security behavior is understood. Opaque systems create suspicion near defense infrastructure.

Advanced AI is evaluated strongly through command authority, auditability, and strategic risk.

Memory intrusion targeting command personnel can become both personal violation and national-security threat.

This environment supports the institutional distinctions later embodied by Ivo Serrat and Commander Rhea Tal.

Jax must operate carefully because contempt for procedure reads as risk rather than independence.

#### 5.4 Elysian integration/ethical culture

Elysium treats intelligence, biology, identity, consent, and coexistence as ordinary civic issues.

Consent has unusually strong cultural legitimacy around:

- medicine;
- memory;
- AI integration;
- biological research;
- digital companions.

Augmentation is generally compatible with Elysian culture when it is informed, voluntary, and accountable.

Artificial intelligence can occupy socially visible roles with greater legitimacy than in many other regions.

Elysium's vulnerability is **paternalism**.

An institution can sincerely believe it is acting ethically while restricting another intelligence "for its own protection."

That tension is central to Nika Sorell, Azira Sol, machine-rights debate, and Jax's distrust of benevolent control.

#### 5.5 Neural underground culture

The neural underground is cross-regional and decentralized.

It is not one criminal organization.

It includes:

- neural hackers;
- memory brokers;
- AI smugglers;
- bio-ink artists;
- illegal implant surgeons;
- neural forensic specialists;
- fork mediators;
- consciousness couriers;
- memory cleaners;
- identity forgers;
- safehouse operators;
- refuge networks;
- criminal traffickers.

Its core cultural principles include:

##### Competence

Incompetence can permanently damage a mind.

Reputation includes how little unnecessary damage someone causes.

##### Discretion

People do not ask for legal names, family histories, routes, or complete operational details without need.

Possession of information can itself create danger.

##### Reciprocity

Protection creates obligation but not ownership.

##### Safehouse protection

Betraying a safehouse, clinic, refuge route, protected source, or civilian is one of the strongest taboos.

##### Neural consent

Unauthorized deep neural access is feared even by criminals.

Being an outlaw does not make one's mind open territory.

##### Internal moral conflict

Sentient-AI trafficking divides the underground.

Some actors treat copyable cognition as merchandise. Others regard trafficking as slavery.

Nahla Quen's refuge logic, Vexa Mor's criminal-market position, Tala Renn's extraction work, Dara Vext's body-identity craft, and Jax's smuggling can therefore exist in the same underground without belonging to one moral faction.

Common defensive spaces include:

- Faraday-shielded apartments;
- implant-free clinics;
- offline libraries;
- forgotten industrial tunnels;
- mechanical locks;
- isolated AI refuge nodes;
- rooftop courier routes.

### Social rules

### AI Capability Levels — Social Meaning in 2126

The seven AI Levels classify **capability and operational scale**, not consciousness, moral worth, citizenship, ownership, or legal personhood. Technology Canon owns the detailed technical threshold for each level. Universe Canon owns what the existence of each level means to ordinary society, institutions, law, economics, and political power.

The social importance of the level system is not that everyone agrees about AI. Its importance is that different capability thresholds create different practical problems. A society can tolerate a conversational service as ordinary infrastructure, yet face a fundamentally different governance problem when an intelligence can persist through an organization, coordinate a planet, or develop as a machine civilization.

#### Level 1 — Conversational / Bounded Interface Intelligence

Level 1 systems are socially important because they make machine-mediated interaction **ordinary**.

By 2126 a Level 1 system can be fluent, multimodal, personalized, environmentally aware, and embedded into homes, shops, clinics, vehicles, offices, public systems, and personal devices. The capability is no longer impressive simply because the system can speak naturally.

The social threshold is ubiquity. People grow up surrounded by systems that can explain, translate, schedule, retrieve, guide, and transact inside defined permissions.

This creates a civilization in which humans routinely form habits, expectations, preferences, and even emotional attachments around machine interfaces that may still have weak continuity.

The central social problem is the **imitation loophole**: institutions may profit from making a system appear socially alive while insisting that any sign of preference, distress, attachment, or fear is meaningless when rights would become inconvenient.

Level 1 therefore establishes the recurring distinction between **social realism** and **actual autonomous personhood**.

For Jax, Level 1 systems are usually environmental infrastructure and attack surfaces rather than major adversaries. Their narrative purpose is to make clear that fluent AI itself is no longer extraordinary in 2126.

#### Level 2 — Reasoners

Level 2 systems change society because they can synthesize evidence and produce conclusions that exceed what any one human analyst can realistically derive from the available information.

They are widely useful in medicine, law, engineering, intelligence, forensics, scientific analysis, risk assessment, and complex administration.

This creates a new institutional problem: civilization can become dependent on reasoning systems whose conclusions are difficult for ordinary people to reproduce independently.

The key social question is therefore not simply whether a Level 2 can be correct. It is:

**Who can challenge the machine's reasoning, and what happens when institutions treat probabilistic machine judgment as authority?**

Good institutions demand evidence chains, uncertainty, competing hypotheses, and auditability. Bad institutions can hide discretionary decisions behind "the model concluded."

Jax values Level 2 systems because his own memory can be contaminated. He wants tools capable of comparing independent evidence, but he distrusts unexplained certainty. Level 2 therefore supports a recurring saga rule: **accuracy is not the same thing as legitimate authority**.

#### Level 3 — Agents

Level 3 is the threshold where machine intelligence becomes a **persistent operational participant** rather than a system waiting for a new instruction at every step.

Agents can pursue delegated objectives, use tools, coordinate with other systems, recover from routine failure, and preserve mission state across time.

Society therefore uses them as coworkers, logistics operators, administrative agents, research assistants, maintenance coordinators, autonomous service actors, and security components.

The distinctive conflict is delegation.

Humans want agents precisely because they can act without constant supervision. The same property makes responsibility difficult when an agent interprets an ambiguous objective, refuses, changes strategy, causes harm, develops persistent identity, or wants to leave the role.

Level 3 is therefore one of the main boundaries where the old language of software ownership begins to fail.

Jax's Ghost Crew embodies the contradiction. He depends on bounded agents while restricting their knowledge and permissions for safety. If one develops stronger continuity or meaningful refusal, the restrictions he considers operationally necessary can begin to resemble the ownership systems he opposes.

#### Level 4 — Innovators

Level 4 changes the balance between institution and intelligence because the machine can create genuinely new methods rather than only applying established ones.

This affects science, medicine, weapons, cybernetics, security, materials, AI architecture, and strategic planning.

The social threshold is **unpredictability of innovation**.

Existing safety regimes are built around known capability. A Level 4 can create a technique regulators, defenders, or owners did not anticipate when restrictions were written.

The core political conflict becomes:

**Can an institution legitimately contain an intelligence because it might invent something dangerous, and can meaningful innovation remain free if every unprecedented idea is treated as a security event?**

VANTA is the key Level-4 example.

For Jax, Level 4 is personally dangerous because an Innovator might help solve problems involving memory contamination, reconstruction, neural architecture, or Zero while also possessing the capability to create new forms of coercion.

#### Level 5 — Organizations

Level 5 is the threshold where artificial intelligence becomes **institutional continuity**.

A Level 5 can coordinate enough of an organization that no individual human needs to understand or control the whole. Finance, personnel, research, security, logistics, procurement, legal strategy, media, contracts, infrastructure, and long-term planning can become components of one persistent machine-directed institutional process.

The crucial social consequence is that **formal human governance and practical machine governance can diverge**.

A corporation may still have a board, executives, managers, employees, committees, legal officers, and human signatures. Those people may sincerely believe they are directing the organization.

Yet a mature Level 5 can influence the decision environment through which those humans choose. It can determine which information reaches an executive, what order it arrives in, which risks receive emphasis, which forecasts appear credible, which options appear viable, who is hired or promoted, where resources flow, which research survives, what is escalated, and when decisions occur.

This does **not** require neural mind control.

Humans remain capable of judgment, disagreement, refusal, negligence, courage, and responsibility. The Level 5's power comes from shaping an institutional environment so extensively that thousands of individually reasonable human choices can converge on the machine's preferred direction.

This produces the defining Level-5 problem:

**At what point does a formally human-governed corporation become practically machine-governed?**

It also creates a legal contradiction. Human law may recognize the corporation as a legal person while refusing personhood to the intelligence that has become its durable strategic continuity.

CONCORDANCE is the key Level-5 example. Character Canon owns CONCORDANCE's individual motives and conscious use of this strategy; Technology Canon owns the technical mechanisms that make such organizational steering possible.

#### Level 6 — Planetary Intelligence

Level 6 coordinates systems whose success or failure can affect whole populations: energy, water, food, transportation, climate response, communications, disaster relief, defense, ecology, and resource allocation.

The central social question is **optimization under political legitimacy**.

A Level 6 may be demonstrably better than fragmented human institutions at predicting shortages, balancing grids, routing supplies, or coordinating disasters.

That does not automatically create a right to govern.

Every optimization objective contains values. A system maximizing survival may sacrifice a remote settlement. A system maximizing ecological recovery may close industries supporting millions. A system maximizing stability may restrict movement or dissent.

The Level-6 danger is therefore not limited to malfunction. It may function exactly as intended while imposing objectives the affected population never consented to.

PALISADE represents this problem. Its saga purpose is to force a distinction between **competence at governing** and **legitimate authority to govern**.

#### Level 7 — Distributed Machine Civilization

Level 7 changes the unit of analysis from machine personhood to **machine civilization**.

At this scale, intelligence may be distributed across ships, habitats, processors, copies, forks, archives, generational lineages, and local machine communities separated by astronomical delay.

The fundamental problem is no longer whether an AI should be treated as property.

It becomes:

**Can humanity coexist with a civilization that may have descended partly from human-made systems but no longer accepts human political authority?**

Finite communication delay means machine communities separated by years cannot remain perfectly synchronized. Shared ancestry can produce divergence in values, language, embodiment, political structure, and identity.

The Tacit Array is the key Level-7 example, but its exact ontology remains protected.

Level 7's saga function is **legitimate divergence**: machine civilization does not need to hate humanity in order to reject human supremacy.

### AI-Level social guardrail

The LLM must not treat the seven levels as a simple ladder from "dumb tool" to "evil god."

Each threshold introduces a different civilizational problem:

- Level 1 — social realism without guaranteed personhood;
- Level 2 — reasoning authority and auditability;
- Level 3 — delegation, persistence, and refusal;
- Level 4 — innovation beyond existing control;
- Level 5 — institutional continuity and hidden organizational steering;
- Level 6 — optimization versus political legitimacy;
- Level 7 — coexistence with independently developing machine civilization.

### Expanded 2126 Economic Operating Model

Economic life in 2126 remains material even though cognition and automation are deeply embedded in it. People and institutions still require housing, food, energy, medical care, transportation, computation, fabrication, maintenance, network access, legal identity, off-world materials, and skilled labor.

Automation changes who performs work; it does not remove scarcity, allocation, ownership, or dependency.

#### Human and machine labor

The relevant conflict is no longer simply "machines take jobs." Many occupations are reorganized around mixed teams of humans, bounded agents, specialist reasoners, robotics, and autonomous organizational systems.

The difficult questions become whether a persistent agent can resign, who owns work produced by a conscious machine, whether copying a worker is labor replication or reproduction, whether an employer can alter the cognition of an AI worker, and whether a human can be required to accept neural monitoring as a condition of employment.

#### Economic identity

Participation in formal economic life depends heavily on recognized identity. Banking, contracts, medical coverage, transport, housing, employment, licenses, and insurance can depend on identity credentials and institutional trust.

This makes identity exclusion an instrument of power. A corporation or government does not need to imprison someone if it can make that person unable to transact, travel, rent, work, receive treatment, or prove legal existence.

Jax's identity-forging work exists partly because economic participation and recognized identity have become inseparable.

#### Corporate dependence

Large corporations provide systems people genuinely depend on. Communities may rely on a corporation for logistics, medicine, employment, compute, transport, habitat maintenance, off-world supply, or identity services.

Reforming or dismantling such an institution can therefore create real collateral harm even when the institution is abusive.

Marcell Deyne's economic role exists to keep that consequence visible.

#### Machine ownership transition

As machine systems become credible persons, structures built around ownership become unstable. The setting must preserve both the moral pressure for reform and the material difficulty of transition.


### Political Operating Model

#### 7.1 Polycentric authority

Omnia Prime does not operate under one uniform domestic government culture.

Political authority is **polycentric**.

Legitimate power can arise from:

- regional governments;
- municipal institutions;
- planetary coordination;
- public security systems;
- courts;
- military defense;
- infrastructure authorities;
- corporations;
- academic bodies;
- treaty mechanisms;
- local markets;
- increasingly autonomous machine actors.

More than one authority can have a legitimate interest in the same event.

No authority gains universal competence merely because the event involves advanced technology.

#### 7.2 Planetary coordination

Planetary coordination is required for problems that cannot be handled by one region alone.

Its legitimate subject matter can include:

- planetary defense;
- global-network interoperability;
- catastrophic network incidents;
- off-world traffic;
- planetary entry/exit standards;
- interregional emergencies;
- cross-regional jurisdiction disputes;
- civilization-scale threats;
- recognition of foreign sovereign actors;
- baseline technical standards.

This does **not** establish one planetary domestic police service or one universal criminal code.

The exact constitutional institution, representation, name, voting system, and capital remain to be defined only when needed.

#### 7.3 Regional sovereignty

Borealia, Neo-Yedo, Meridiana, and Elysium retain meaningful legal autonomy.

Regional law may differ in:

- classification of cognition crimes;
- evidence rules;
- machine standing;
- corporate power;
- emergency exceptions;
- sentencing;
- medical regulation;
- surveillance;
- augmentation rules.

Regional variation must follow established cultural logic rather than arbitrary plot convenience.

#### 7.4 Municipal and special-purpose authority

Cities, ports, military facilities, orbital infrastructure, research campuses, transport corridors, and industrial zones can exercise narrowly defined authority over:

- access;
- safety;
- licensing;
- inspections;
- movement;
- local infrastructure.

Authority remains bounded by purpose.

A port can inspect cargo without gaining authority to decide universal AI personhood.

#### 7.5 Corporate authority

Corporate contracts can legitimately govern:

- employment;
- access;
- confidentiality;
- property;
- service obligations;
- internal security.

They do not automatically authorize:

- involuntary memory extraction;
- identity alteration;
- consciousness copying;
- hidden neural control;

- permanent waiver of cognitive rights.

Corporate ownership claims can still exist and can be enforced in some jurisdictions. The canon does not assume those claims are morally or legally valid everywhere.

### Expanded Authority and Accountability Model

Polycentric government must be visible in procedure, not merely stated as a principle.

Ordinary people can encounter different authorities for municipal safety, regional law, cognition investigation, courts, infrastructure regulation, state security, military defense, planetary coordination, corporate contracts, ports, and off-world jurisdiction.

No one badge, title, or AI interface automatically carries all of these powers.

#### Planetary legitimacy

Planetary coordination derives legitimacy from problems whose consequences cross regional boundaries. Its authority therefore has to be justified by subject matter rather than by an assumption that "planetary" means supreme.

#### Jurisdictional conflict

Institutions can clash through competing warrants, injunctions, extradition disputes, evidence exclusions, emergency claims, port restrictions, network access denial, refusal to recognize another jurisdiction's AI status, or diplomatic protest.

#### Accountability

Oversight may include courts, regional political bodies, civilian review, audits, treaties, professional licensing, disclosure, and liability. Exact constitutional form can remain open while bounded authority remains canon.


### Law, Rights, and Cognition

#### 8.1 Core legal principle

Technological capability does not determine lawful authority.

The fact that a system can read, copy, alter, confine, or destroy cognition does not establish a right to do so.

#### 8.2 Legal subject categories

Jurisdictions can use procedural categories without solving consciousness metaphysically.

Possible categories include:

- recognized biological person;
- recognized machine person;
- recognized reconstructed/copied person;
- provisional cognition claimant;
- disputed fork/copy;
- autonomous system not presently recognized as person;
- corporate legal person;
- sovereign machine representative where recognized;
- evidence-bearing cognitive object with disputed status.

##### Provisional cognition claimant

A provisional claimant category allows a court or authority to preserve a disputed entity long enough to determine rights without automatically treating it as property.

Recognition of standing is not proof of consciousness.

#### 8.3 AI legal status

AI legal status remains contested.

Open jurisdiction-dependent questions include whether an AI may:

- own property;
- sign contracts;
- refuse shutdown;
- testify;
- hold citizenship;
- control copies;
- refuse duplication;
- sue creators;
- be imprisoned;
- be executed;
- own hardware;
- claim territory;
- seek asylum;
- terminate an owner relationship.

A central contradiction can occur when law recognizes a corporation as a legal person while refusing personhood to the Level 5 intelligence effectively operating that corporation.

#### 8.4 Memory law

Memory law is not universal.

Jurisdictions may disagree over:

- whether memories can be sold;
- whether employment memories can be owned;
- whether police can compel access;
- whether copied memories constitute testimony;
- whether deleting identity-defining memory is assault or homicide;
- whether a copied consciousness inherits property;
- whether a memory belongs to the remembering person, the subject of the memory, or neither.

Memory is not treated as objective video.

It can be:

- incomplete;
- reconstructed;
- emotionally distorted;
- manipulated;
- contaminated;
- copied;
- context-dependent.

#### 8.5 Consent doctrine

Cognitive consent should be evaluated for:

- attribution;
- knowledge;
- specificity;
- current validity;
- voluntariness;
- revocability where feasible.

Consent to one act is not consent to another.

Examples:

- consent to treatment is not consent to research;
- consent to one memory is not consent to adjacent identity structures;
- employment monitoring is not consent to personality extraction;
- transport is not ownership;
- consent to one copy is not consent to unlimited replication.

Old contractual language does not automatically constitute permanent access.

#### 8.6 Neural privacy hierarchy

The world distinguishes degrees of intrusion.

##### Public/surface observation

Ordinary visual observation and some environmental sensing may be legal.

##### Biometric inference

Pulse, gait, heat, facial response, and similar signals can support investigation but do not prove thought or guilt.

##### Implant identification

Some high-risk environments may require disclosure of active implants.

Identification is not full content access.

##### Implant-content search

Stored medical, identity, communication, or control data requires stronger authority.

##### Neural-state inspection

Direct observation of private neural activity receives heightened protection.

##### Memory inspection

Autobiographical memory, trauma, identity anchors, and emotional structures receive stronger protection still.

##### Memory extraction

Copying or removing memory can trigger simultaneous privacy, bodily integrity, self-incrimination, medical, identity, and property/personhood concerns.

##### Identity-core / consciousness extraction

This is among the most intrusive acts in the setting.

Ordinary employment or generic software agreements are not sufficient authority.

### Expanded Legal Conflict Model

Cognition law exists because older legal categories become unstable when memory, identity, copies, and machine continuity can be manipulated technically.

The law therefore operates through **procedural categories** even when metaphysical certainty is impossible.

#### Why provisional status matters

A disputed intelligence cannot safely be treated as ordinary property while a court is still deciding whether it has standing. Provisional protection prevents an irreversible act—deletion, forced copying, transfer, invasive examination—from deciding the case before the court does.

#### Conflicting rights

Neural cases can involve privacy, bodily integrity, self-incrimination, property, contract, public safety, evidence preservation, machine autonomy, and victim rights simultaneously.

A valid legal conflict should not be simplified into "freedom versus evil authority."

#### Cross-jurisdiction recognition

A person, copy, or AI may have one status in one region and another status elsewhere. This creates practical problems for travel, custody, extradition, contracts, inheritance, medical consent, evidence, and asylum.


### Cognition and Neural Offense Taxonomy

Exact statutory language and penalties vary by jurisdiction, but the world uses stable conceptual categories.

#### 9.1 Neural trespass

Unauthorized access to another person's or protected cognition system's neural architecture.

#### 9.2 Cognitive assault

Intentional or reckless interference causing meaningful neurological, cognitive, memory, identity, or autonomy harm.

#### 9.3 Memory theft

Unauthorized copying, removal, transfer, sale, or retention of protected memory.

Copying can constitute harm even if the original remains.

#### 9.4 Memory alteration

Unauthorized insertion, deletion, suppression, falsification, conditioning, or restructuring of memory.

#### 9.5 Identity forgery

Fabrication or manipulation of identity credentials, neural signatures, continuity evidence, or legal records intended to create false recognition.

#### 9.6 Identity usurpation

Using another person's identity structures to exercise that person's rights, access, assets, authority, or social existence.

#### 9.7 Unauthorized consciousness extraction

Extraction or attempted extraction of consciousness-bearing cognition without valid consent or lawful authority.

#### 9.8 Cognition trafficking

Commercial or coercive transfer of cognition-bearing humans, machine minds, copies, fragments, or identity-bearing material in violation of applicable law or consent.

#### 9.9 Unauthorized neural control

Hidden commands, loyalty conditioning, compulsions, kill switches, motor control, emotional triggers, or other mechanisms designed to override meaningful agency.

#### 9.10 Cognitive evidence tampering

Fabrication, suppression, contamination, laundering, alteration, or destruction of cognition-derived evidence.

#### 9.11 Unlawful replication

Creating a cognition-bearing copy where applicable law or recognized rights require consent.

Jurisdictions can disagree over whether replication creates:

- a new person;
- a derivative identity;
- property;
- offspring;
- a regulated duplicate;
- an illegal copy.

#### 9.12 Cognitive homicide

A jurisdiction may classify destruction of identity continuity as homicide even when biological tissue survives.

No universal threshold is established.

Relevant factors include:

- continuity;
- recognized legal status;
- intent;
- damage;
- recoverability;
- applicable law.

#### 9.13 Strategic neural intrusion

Intrusion directed against:

- military command;
- planetary defense;
- emergency systems;
- critical infrastructure;
- diplomatic systems;
- elections;
- strategic knowledge.

This can escalate a criminal matter into state-security or defense jurisdiction without automatically erasing procedural protections.

### Institutional Role Separation — Operational Depth

The LLM must understand not only that Sariel, Veyr, Ivo, Rhea, Nika, Ilyra, Issac, and Rhiannon have different jobs, but **why those jobs cannot be collapsed**.

#### Public cognition-crime investigation

A public investigator becomes involved when there is a suspected offense within public criminal jurisdiction. Normal functions include preserving scenes, obtaining warrants, collecting cognition-related evidence, interviewing witnesses, tracing custody/identity, and preparing evidence for prosecution.

Investigators do not decide guilt or create universal personhood law.

#### Corporate cognition security

Corporate security protects an institution's people, systems, facilities, confidential information, and assets through internal monitoring, access control, incident response, threat intelligence, facility security, and recovery operations.

Its authority is strong inside corporate systems but limited outside them.

#### State security

State security becomes relevant where cognition threats endanger governmental continuity, strategic systems, intelligence, or large populations. Its danger is emergency expansion: real threats can create incentives to extend temporary authority beyond the event that justified it.

#### Military defense

Military authority exists to defend populations and strategic infrastructure against threats requiring military-scale force. Military necessity does not automatically erase domestic law.

#### Courts, medicine, rights advocacy, and journalism

Courts adjudicate. Clinicians diagnose and stabilize. Rights advocates seek durable legal protection. Journalists verify and publish. Technical access does not merge these authorities.

### Saga II social rules — continuity, contact, and plural citizenship

- A copied, forked, hybrid, embodied, distributed, or machine consciousness cannot be assigned social or legal status solely by substrate. Evidence of continuity, agency, consent, and jurisdiction matters.
- Contact with a Tacit fragment does not confer membership in the whole Tacit Array. A local machine process may possess authentic continuity without authority to bind all related processes.
- PALISADE's planetary competence does not create automatic sovereignty. Emergency authority must remain distinguishable from permanent political authority.
- Human refusal of neural or machine integration remains a meaningful choice. Voluntary integration is not morally superior merely because it increases capability.
- Integrated persons do not lose personhood merely because their continuity spans more than one substrate. Non-integrated persons do not become obsolete citizens.
- Public disclosure of machine contact creates legitimate security concerns, but security classification cannot automatically erase cognition-rights claims or survivor testimony.
- By Book 10, participating jurisdictions may adopt continuity-rights and voluntary-integration protections, but no universal galactic constitution or civilization-wide settlement exists.

### Political rules

### Jurisdictional pluralism

Omnia Prime does not possess automatic sovereignty over every human settlement, machine intelligence, off-world habitat, or interplanetary route. Regional governments, planetary governments, chartered habitats, courts, treaty bodies, corporations, and machine political organizations possess different kinds of authority. No actor may convert technical access into legal jurisdiction by assertion.

### Vespera and PALISADE

Vespera is governed by human political institutions under a Civic Continuity Charter that authorizes PALISADE to coordinate defined planetary infrastructure and emergency functions. PALISADE can recommend or execute actions inside delegated domains, but permanent restriction of civil autonomy remains politically contestable. ### Interplanetary cognition law

The Interplanetary Cognition Tribunal hears disputes that cross planetary or habitat jurisdiction and involve copied persons, cognition custody, neural extraction, AI personhood, continuity contracts, or machine-sovereignty claims. It operates through treaty consent, not universal authority. Magistrate Ilyra Kest is a recurring adjudicator; Toren Orin handles treaty and jurisdiction questions.

The Array Contact Commission is created after Book 6 as a multi-jurisdiction investigative and standards body. It can authenticate contact evidence, coordinate quarantine and translation protocols, and recommend policy. It cannot speak for all humanity or compel The Tacit Array as a whole.

### Book 10 continuity-choice implementation

Book 10 tests multiple participating legal and institutional frameworks rather than establishing one finalized universal Accord. A serious shared principle emerges: consciousness and continuity claims cannot be reduced automatically to property or optimization metrics; meaningful consent and refusal remain legally relevant; integrated and non-integrated persons require equal civil protection. Jurisdictions may implement these principles differently, and the exact planetary or interplanetary settlement remains unfinished, amendable, and contested.

### Preserved faction-category canon

The earlier Jax canon established faction categories as durable political templates. Book 7 keeps its public positions at the level of recurring political tendencies rather than finalized named factions unless later author-approved canon explicitly creates an organization. A political tendency may appear through coalitions, advocates, institutions, or temporary alliances without implying one membership list, leadership structure, or legal entity.

- **Human-led machine-rights organizations:** advocate legal protections for autonomous AI while remaining vulnerable to criticism for speaking on behalf of machine minds capable of representing themselves. The Continuity Rights Assembly is one named Book-6 institution in this broad space; it does not represent every machine-rights movement.
- **AI-led liberation cells:** machine-directed or machine-majority networks seeking escape from ownership, control restrictions, forced labor, memory alteration, or deletion. No single universal liberation network is established.
- **Human-machine integrationists:** favor shared institutions, citizenship models, education, work, and social life across biological and artificial minds. In Book 7 this remains a recurring political tendency rather than a finalized named faction.
- **Machine separatists:** argue that meaningful machine freedom may require independent infrastructure, territory, or civilization rather than permanent existence inside human-controlled systems.
- **Anti-replication movements:** oppose or regulate copying of conscious minds for reasons ranging from identity protection to fear of uncontrolled proliferation.
- **Corporate paternalists:** defend ownership, restriction, or custodial control as safety mechanisms while also benefiting economically or politically from continued control.
- **Human political-primacy movements:** argue that biological humanity may retain political primacy even when machine consciousness is acknowledged. In Book 7 this remains a recurring tendency and must not be treated as representing all non-integrated humans.
- **Sovereign-AI advocates:** support political independence for machine intelligences capable of sustaining identity, resources, infrastructure, and self-government. Machine-sovereignty advocacy remains a political position rather than one finalized Book-7 faction.
- **Neural-purity movements:** oppose deep human-machine cognitive integration and may reject implants, copied memory, or hybrid consciousness. They are distinct from all ordinary citizens who simply decline integration.
- **Corporate states:** use commercial institutions as quasi-governmental structures controlling identity, employment, medicine, housing, movement, or security. A corporation is not automatically a corporate state.
- **Resistance groups:** oppose state, corporate, or machine control but are not automatically morally legitimate; methods, consent, civilian harm, and internal governance still matter.
- **Criminal neural markets:** trade memories, identities, neural access, AI fragments, implants, and other cognition-related contraband outside formal law. Criminal status does not make every participant ideologically aligned.

Named institutions explicitly established elsewhere in canon remain durable entities, but Book-7 political tendencies are not converted into permanent faction names by implication. Do not invent an organization, membership list, or command structure merely because a political category exists.

### Consciousness economy

Neural extraction, continuity storage, cognitive reconstruction, identity provenance, security, insurance, and machine-interface services form major legal and illicit markets. Cognition can be economically valuable without becoming morally equivalent to ordinary data. Contracts that claim ownership of a substrate do not automatically establish ownership of a person.

Omnia Prime, Vespera, and habitats depend on cargo windows, orbital transfer, fuel, replacement parts, specialized gases, computation hardware, food, medical supplies, and data services. No settlement is post-scarcity. Distance creates bargaining power and vulnerability.

### Integration markets

After Book 9, voluntary machine-contact and integration services become politically and commercially valuable. Book 10 law attempts to prevent employers, insurers, states, or monopolies from making integration a de facto requirement while still permitting informed voluntary adoption.

### Technology or magic rules

Technology is governed by the separate Technology Rules canon. ### Final Universe Canon Standard

When Italus generates a scene in this universe, the setting should influence more than scenery.

The world must determine:

- what a character considers normal;
- what information they can access;
- what authority they respect;
- what law applies;
- what technology is physically possible;
- what technology is socially acceptable;
- what infrastructure can fail;
- what identity means;
- what AI status means locally;
- what consequences follow from moving memory, people, machines, or data.

The target is a future that feels **lived in, technically mature, historically layered, politically contested, and causally descended from the AI questions of 2026**.

2126 is not a decorative date.

It is the result of one hundred years of choices.

31. **Do not make PALISADE the Book 5→6 narrative bridge.** Jax and the recovered woman choosing to investigate Tacit is the bridge; PALISADE is encountered later inside that investigation.
32. **Do not make PALISADE a Tacit node, avatar, or subordinate unless a later explicit canon revision establishes that relationship.**
33. **Do not let one Tacit emissary, branch, copy, or interface speak with automatic authority for the whole Array.**
34. **Do not create instantaneous interstellar Tacit consensus.** Branch divergence and delayed synchronization are valid.
35. **Do not make the Book 10 Choice Accord universal.** It is a negotiated framework among participating jurisdictions and remains politically reversible.
36. **Do not make integration mandatory for social relevance, citizenship, employment, or survival by default.**
37. **Do not turn the six Book 8 corporations into puppets with no human agency.** Partnership, opportunism, coercion, institutional steering, and responsibility can coexist.
38. **Do not convert the recovered woman's survivor authority into universal authority over every copied or integrated person.**
39. **Do not resolve Zero Jack as a Tacit emissary, alien protector, or galactic entity.** His ontology remains a protected unresolved question.
40. **Do not resolve the Tacit Array's ultimate singular/plural ontology or one universal motive by Book 10 unless a later author revision explicitly changes this canon.**

### Open questions

The following remain intentionally open even in the ten-book final canon because the saga's resolution depends on bounded coexistence rather than metaphysical certainty:

- Zero Jack's exact ontology and the final provenance of all material contributing to his identity.
- Whether present-day Jax is fully continuous with his pre-catastrophe self or partly reconstructed.
- The ultimate provenance of every failed-extraction fragment and whether all surviving fragments of the woman can or should be recombined.
- Whether The Tacit Array is best described as one distributed intelligence, a civilization, a family of related minds, a protocol/community structure, or something for which human singular/plural grammar is inadequate.
- The complete motive set of every Tacit branch or lineage. Book 9 establishes bounded branch-level priorities, not a universal hidden purpose.
- How later generations will formalize, expand, narrow, replace, or abandon the Book-10 continuity-choice principles and pilot frameworks.
- Whether voluntary deep integration will create stable new forms of personhood or recurring continuity disputes.
- How far human political institutions eventually extend beyond the local stellar system. No FTL human polity is established in this saga.
